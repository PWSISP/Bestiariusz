# S04-04 — Icons of the five elements

File to edit: src/view/element-icons.ts

Goal: next to the cost of a card and in the status bar the game draws a small icon of each element (fire, water, earth, wind, thunder), filled with the colour of the element. An icon is a list of polygons in the unit square (0 … 1 in both directions, y grows downwards). You draw the five icons as data and write two small helpers. Read `src/view/geometry.ts` (`Point`, `Rect`) first.

Signatures (do not change): `IconPolygon`, `ElementIcon`, `ELEMENT_ICONS`, `FALLBACK_ICON`, `iconFor` and `placeIcon` in the stub.

Rules for the data (`ELEMENT_ICONS` has exactly the keys `ogien`, `woda`, `ziemia`, `wiatr`, `grom`):
1. An icon has 1 to 4 polygons; a polygon has 3 to 24 points; every coordinate is a finite number from 0 to 1 (inclusive); the area of a polygon is at least 0.01; no polygon crosses itself (two edges that are not neighbours never touch or cross).
2. The test fills each icon on a 48 × 48 grid (a cell is filled when the centre of the cell is inside any polygon of the icon, a polygon is "inside" by the even-odd rule). The filled part is between 15% and 60% of the grid, and for any two different elements the overlap (filled in both) divided by the union (filled in either) is below 0.7. So the five icons must look different.
3. `ogien` is a flame: the filled part reaches above y = 0.15 and below y = 0.85; a row of cells near the bottom (y = 0.8) is at least twice as wide as a row near the top (y = 0.2); the icon is not left–right symmetric (its overlap with its own mirror image, divided by the union, is below 0.9).
4. `woda` is a drop: reaches above y = 0.15 and below y = 0.85; is left–right symmetric (overlap with its mirror image divided by union is at least 0.9); every row of cells with y below 0.2 is at most 0.2 wide (a sharp tip); the centre of the filled part is below y = 0.5.
5. `ziemia` is a mountain: nothing is filled above y = 0.3 wider than 0.25 per row; a row at y = 0.8 is at least 0.6 wide; the filled part reaches below y = 0.85 and reaches above y = 0.3; the centre of the filled part is below y = 0.5.
6. `wiatr` is wind, several separate gusts: scanning the rows from top to bottom, there are at least 3 separate groups of rows with at least 4 filled cells each, separated by rows with fewer; the filled part is at least 0.7 wide (leftmost to rightmost filled cell).
7. `grom` is a bolt of lightning: the filled part is at least 0.85 tall; its overlap with its mirror image divided by union is below 0.7; the filled part is less than 80% of its convex hull (the smallest convex polygon around all points of the icon is more than 1.25 times its area).

Rules for the helpers:
8. `FALLBACK_ICON` is exactly one polygon with the points (0.5, 0.1), (0.9, 0.5), (0.5, 0.9), (0.1, 0.5), in this order: a diamond.
9. `iconFor(element)` returns `ELEMENT_ICONS[element]` (the same object) for the five elements and `FALLBACK_ICON` for every other string, including names that exist on every JavaScript object (`toString`, `constructor`, `__proto__`) and the empty string.
10. `placeIcon(icon, box)` puts the unit square into the largest square that fits in `box`, centred in `box`: `side = min(box.width, box.height)`, left = `box.x + (box.width - side) / 2`, top = `box.y + (box.height - side) / 2`, and every point `(x, y)` becomes `(left + x * side, top + y * side)`. It returns new arrays with new points in the same order; the icon is not modified. A box with width or height 0 returns polygons whose points are all the same point (no error).

Edge cases:
- `placeIcon(FALLBACK_ICON, { x: 10, y: 20, width: 100, height: 40 })` returns one polygon with the points (60, 24), (76, 40), (60, 56), (44, 40): the square has side 40, left = 40, top = 20.
- `placeIcon` of an icon with two polygons returns two polygons in the same order.
